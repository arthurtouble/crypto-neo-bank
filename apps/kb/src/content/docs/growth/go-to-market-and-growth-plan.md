## 1. Executive decision

Aurel should launch as an **invite-only financial operating system for people who already live partly onchain**, not as a mass-market “crypto bank.” The first wedge is the customer who receives, holds, or manages meaningful stablecoin and onchain balances and wants a safer, clearer way to operate them.

The acquisition model should be trust-led and product-led:

```text
Useful insight or tool
  -> qualified application
  -> human-reviewed invitation
  -> guided, small-value first success
  -> demonstrated safety and support
  -> retained funded relationship
  -> tightly controlled referral
```

The first 100 customers should come primarily from high-trust, low-volume channels. Broad performance marketing should wait until Aurel has an approved launch entity and countries, a contracted regulated provider, reliable contribution-margin data, and evidence that invited users activate and retain.

### The strategic choices

1. **Use a qualified waitlist, not an open signup or a referral leaderboard.** Aurel needs the right customers in approved countries, not the largest email list.
2. **Run three deliberately small cohorts: 5, 25, then 100.** Expansion is gated by product, safety, operations, and retention evidence rather than a launch date.
3. **Lead with control, clarity, and one coherent view.** Do not lead with APY, cashback, a metal card, or “banking” language.
4. **Make founder-led distribution and ecosystem partnerships the primary channels.** These are the best match for a high-trust, high-value early product.
5. **Use content to create trust and demand, not content volume.** Human expertise and real product demonstrations should be the source; AI can research, repurpose, edit, localize, and measure.
6. **Build a few useful public utilities.** A read-only wallet/treasury checkup, risk receipt, and stablecoin workflow guides can create compounding inbound while demonstrating the product's philosophy.
7. **Do not hire a full-service marketing agency yet.** Use a fractional growth owner plus specialist production support until product-market fit and compliant paid distribution are proven.

## 2. What the product can honestly sell now

### Live or implemented

- Privy authentication and customer-controlled embedded/external wallets.
- Live Base ETH, USDC, and WETH reads.
- User-confirmed sends, LI.FI cross-chain routing, Aave Earn, collateralized borrowing, and repayment preparation.
- Visible policies, transaction simulation, limits, destination controls, and recovery/security surfaces.
- Unified portfolio, activity, membership projection, support intake, feedback, status, and trust documentation.
- Invitation, cohort, geography, transaction-cap, kill-switch, and beta-operations controls.

### Not ready to promise in acquisition

- Bank accounts, fiat conversion, cards, and funded benefits are provider-gated.
- Initial and excluded countries have not been approved.
- Launch and contracting entities are undecided.
- Several independent security, legal, support, incident, and edge-security gates remain open.
- The public landing page currently says “Open Aurel” and sends users into a preview/login flow; it does not collect a qualified waitlist application.
- Product analytics begin after authentication. There is no durable top-of-funnel attribution, campaign, referral, consented lifecycle messaging, or public acquisition experiment model.

The near-term promise should therefore be:

> See and operate your onchain wealth with visible control, risk, and support.

The longer-term vision can be stated separately:

> Receive digital dollars, keep selected balances productive, and use your money globally from one coherent account—when each service is available and approved for you.

## 3. Ideal customer profile and launch beachhead

### Primary ICP: the stablecoin operator

A globally mobile founder, independent professional, investor, or small-company principal who:

- has roughly $25,000–$500,000 in digital assets;
- receives income, distributions, client payments, or treasury flows in stablecoins;
- uses two or more wallets, exchanges, chains, or DeFi protocols;
- cares more about control, support, and operational clarity than speculative trading;
- is comfortable completing identity checks for regulated services;
- will test a new product with a modest balance; and
- can become a high-value paid member if the product saves time or reduces operating risk.

### First three use-case segments

| Priority | Segment | Trigger | First job Aurel solves | Where to reach them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Crypto-paid founders and professionals | Stablecoin income is fragmented and awkward to use | Receive, see, protect, and move funds | Founder communities, crypto payroll/accounting partners, referrals |
| 2 | Onchain investors with fragmented positions | No coherent risk and liquidity view | Aggregate balances, surface risk, prepare safe actions | Base/Aave/DeFi communities, research content, wallet checkup |
| 3 | Small-company principals and DAOs | Treasury controls rely on spreadsheets and manual approvals | Visibility, policies, roles, reporting, support | CFO/accountant networks, Safe/treasury communities, targeted outbound |

Do not begin with novice retail investors, high-frequency traders, yield tourists, anonymous/KYC-averse users, or customers whose main motivation is cashback. They create weak retention, higher support or compliance cost, and pressure Aurel to compete on unsustainable subsidies.

### Customer research before scale

Complete 30 structured interviews before Cohort 2:

- 10 stablecoin-paid founders/professionals;
- 10 onchain investors with at least two wallets/protocols;
- 5 accountants, fractional CFOs, or crypto tax professionals;
- 5 users who fit the wealth profile but are cautious about self-custody.

Capture current workflow, frequency, cost, failure anxiety, existing products, switching trigger, trust requirements, willingness to pay, and the smallest action they would trust Aurel to help perform. Do not ask whether they “like the idea”; ask for the last real workflow and its consequences.

## 4. Positioning and message system

### Category

**Financial operating system for onchain wealth**

Use “crypto neo-bank” internally as a market shorthand only. Publicly, “bank,” “account,” “deposit,” “savings,” insurance, and yield language must match the approved legal and provider structure.

### Message hierarchy

1. **One clear view:** see wallets, stablecoins, positions, and obligations together.
2. **You stay in control:** customers sign every transaction and can understand how to leave.
3. **Risk before action:** costs, custody, liquidity, counterparties, and failure modes appear before confirmation.
4. **Money that works across contexts:** receive, move, allocate, and eventually spend through approved services.
5. **Human help when money is stuck:** operational support is part of the product, not an FAQ afterthought.

### Proof points

- No house token.
- No internal authoritative balance ledger.
- Live onchain reads and source-linked activity.
- Explicit signing, simulation, limits, allowlists, cooling periods, recovery, and account lock.
- Public provider responsibility, fee, risk, status, and incident documentation.
- Small cohorts with direct support and published limits.

### Claims to avoid

- “A bank,” “bank account,” or “private bank” before the structure supports it.
- “Safe,” “risk-free,” “protected,” or “insured” without precise qualification.
- “Guaranteed,” “passive income,” “savings rate,” or a headline APY.
- “Best return,” “highest yield,” or personalized recommendations without an approved advice framework.
- “Any asset,” “any chain,” “instant,” or “free” when support, settlement, or fees vary.
- Future cards, fiat rails, benefits, and tokenized markets presented as currently available.

### Landing-page change

Replace the primary pre-launch call to action from **Open Aurel** to **Apply for private access**. Keep a secondary **Explore the product** route for a clearly labeled, non-transactional tour. The application should set expectations: small cohorts, approved countries only, no deposit request, real mainnet risk, and direct onboarding support.

## 5. Launch architecture

### Phase 0 — readiness and demand discovery

Audience: public site plus interviews; no broad product solicitation.  
Goal: determine the best first workflow and collect qualified, consented demand.

Required work:

- Close or assign every launch-readiness gate.
- Approve the entity, countries, product characterization, terms, claims library, and partner responsibilities.
- Add a supported-country application/waitlist with consent and first-touch/last-touch attribution.
- Recruit 5 internal testers and 20–30 customer-research participants manually.
- Establish a launch claims register: claim, evidence, countries, approver, version, and expiry/review date.
- Record baseline interview language and objections before rewriting the site.

Exit: legal permission for the exact beta, five successful acceptance testers, named operations coverage, and no critical/high unresolved issue.

### Phase 1 — Cohort 0: five acceptance users

Goal: verify that people unfamiliar with the implementation can complete setup, recovery, test funding, a small action, and withdrawal without coaching.

- Every user gets a 30-minute kickoff and a named contact.
- Observe sessions with consent; distinguish product confusion from trust objections.
- No referral request.
- Fix issues before adding users.

Exit: the existing acceptance and security gates, plus a clear, repeatable “first value” moment.

### Phase 2 — Cohort 1: 25 founding clients

Goal: prove activation, first success, supportability, and four-week retention.

Acquisition mix:

- 10 founder-network referrals;
- 5 ecosystem/technology-partner introductions;
- 5 accountant, CFO, payroll, or legal-community introductions;
- 5 qualified inbound applicants from content or tools.

Offer:

- founding-client access;
- a guided wallet and security setup;
- an optional read-only “onchain operating review”;
- direct support and product council access;
- no guaranteed future benefits and no cash referral bounty.

Exit: all repository beta gates for two consecutive weeks, at least 70% of accepted invites reaching the defined activation event, at least 50% four-week retained among activated customers, and evidence that at least one segment repeats a meaningful workflow. These are operating thresholds to test, not external industry benchmarks.

### Phase 3 — Cohort 2: 100 controlled external users

Goal: find a repeatable acquisition path and establish early unit economics.

- Open 20–25 invitations per week, not all at once.
- Give activated, retained, low-risk customers one or two named invitations.
- Run partner-specific cohorts with unique attribution codes.
- Add creator pilots only with pre-approved scripts/claims, clear sponsorship disclosure, and no compensation based on deposited assets or trading volume.
- Begin lifecycle email/in-app guidance only for consented users.

Exit: one channel produces 20+ activated users with stable quality, six-to-eight-week retention holds, support and risk costs are understood, and paid membership or another disclosed revenue line has real conversion evidence.

### Phase 4 — public gated launch

This is not “everyone can transact.” It is a public application with fast access for eligible, well-understood segments. Launch by country and capability. Keep waitlist and kill-switch controls.

A public launch requires every security, operations, Cloudflare, provider, and legal gate in [launch readiness](/overview/launch-readiness/) to have evidence and an accountable owner.

## 6. Waitlist and invitation design

### Application fields

Collect only what is needed:

- email and country;
- primary job: receive, see, protect, earn, spend, move, or company treasury;
- current stablecoin/onchain workflow frequency;
- broad asset band, optional and clearly explained;
- wallet/chains used;
- individual, family, or company use;
- desired first outcome;
- referral or discovery source;
- consent to beta communications, separate from general marketing consent.

Do not collect wallet addresses, source-of-funds documents, or deposits on the public waitlist.

### Prioritization

Score applications internally on:

- approved geography;
- fit with a supported workflow;
- current pain/frequency;
- ability to test safely with a small amount;
- cohort learning value;
- support capacity.

Never sell queue position. Do not show a public numerical rank. Referral count can be one input after fit, never the overriding input. A leaderboard attracts incentive hunters and makes controlled compliance cohorts harder.

### Referral loop

Unlock referrals only after a customer completes setup, a safe first action, and a short satisfaction/trust check. Give one or two named invitations, expiring in 14 days. Reward successful advocacy with access, recognition, a product council session, or a cost-capped service—not token rewards, uncapped cashback, or payments based on assets deposited.

Measure:

```text
invite share rate
  x invite acceptance rate
  x referred-user activation rate
  = referral coefficient
```

Quality matters more than a coefficient above one. Compare 30/60/90-day retention, support cost, and risk flags for referred versus non-referred cohorts.

## 7. Channel strategy

### Ranked channel portfolio

| Rank | Channel | Why it fits | Cash CAC | Main risk | First experiment |
|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Founder-led referrals and targeted outreach | Trust transfer and precise qualification | Very low | Founder time; small scale | 50 named prospects, 20 conversations, 10 qualified applications |
| 2 | Accountants, crypto tax firms, fractional CFOs, payroll and treasury partners | They see the workflow pain at the moment it occurs | Low | Referral conflicts; slow enablement | 10 partner interviews, 3 pilot cohorts, co-authored workflow guide |
| 3 | Base, Privy, Aave, LI.FI, wallet and security ecosystems | Product proof and concentrated ICP access | Low | Dependence on partner attention | Integration case study, office hour, ecosystem listing, joint demo |
| 4 | Customer referral | Highest-trust compounding loop after value is proven | Low | Incentive abuse and poor-fit friends | Two named invitations after activation and retention checkpoint |
| 5 | Founder-led educational content | Builds trust, gives every outbound message proof, compounds | Low cash, high time | Generic crypto content; compliance errors | One weekly source memo repurposed into three short videos and five posts |
| 6 | Public utilities and programmatic education | Demonstrates value before signup and captures high-intent search | Low to medium | Advice characterization; maintenance | Read-only wallet/treasury checkup with an application CTA |
| 7 | Niche communities and private events | Strong context and candid feedback | Low to medium | Spam/reputation damage | Small “onchain money operations” clinic for 8–12 people |
| 8 | Podcasts, newsletters, and creator partnerships | Borrowed trust and durable explanation | Medium | Expensive impressions; claims by third parties | Five small, technical creators on fixed-fee pilots |
| 9 | PR | Useful for milestones and partner credibility | Variable | Empty launch coverage; low conversion | Security review, regulated partner, or cohort evidence—not a waitlist press release |
| 10 | Paid search and retargeting | Captures existing intent after messaging works | Medium/high | Certification, licensing, weak economics | Branded/high-intent search only after approval |
| 11 | Broad paid social | Scale only after product/market and creative fit | High/uncertain | Platform restrictions, fraud, poor lead quality | Do not run during initial cohorts |

### Channel playbooks

#### Founder-led outbound

Build a list of 100 people or organizations, each with a real trigger: crypto payroll, recent treasury raise, multi-wallet operations, international contracting, or visible DeFi use. The message offers a 20-minute workflow teardown or a founding-client interview—not “join our waitlist.” Track introductions, meetings, qualified applications, invitations, activation, and retention by source.

#### Professional referrers

Create a one-page partner brief for accountants, tax professionals, fractional CFOs, startup counsel, and crypto payroll platforms:

- who Aurel is and is not for;
- current versus future capabilities;
- provider and custody boundaries;
- permitted claims;
- handoff and support process;
- conflict/referral disclosure;
- data-sharing limits; and
- partner-specific invitation code.

Prefer non-exclusive, educational partnerships. Do not pay a percentage of balances, yield, trades, or borrowing.

#### Ecosystem distribution

Aurel is Base-first and directly integrates Privy, Aave, and LI.FI. Each integration can produce a technically credible artifact: integration story, safety teardown, office hour, documentation contribution, and partner directory listing. Base currently promotes ecosystem listings and builder programs, while its 2026 strategy explicitly emphasizes stablecoin payments and global onchain finance. Treat grants or amplification as upside, not the acquisition plan.

#### Communities

Earn permission before posting. Answer operational questions with concrete diagrams, checklists, and tools. Good venues are focused founder/CFO groups, Base and Aave builder/user communities, Safe treasury communities, crypto tax groups, and geographically approved web3 communities. Avoid drop-in link promotion, token communities unrelated to the product, and paid shill groups.

#### Creators

Start with 5–10 smaller creators whose audiences are founders, self-custody users, stablecoin earners, or treasury operators. Use fixed-fee pilots plus a cost-capped qualified-activation bonus only if counsel approves it. Require sponsorship disclosures, pre-approved factual claims, access to the product before endorsement, and an archive of the published asset. Never script personal investment outcomes.

#### Paid acquisition

Paid channels are a later amplifier. Google currently requires EU advertisers promoting software wallets or exchanges to be an authorized MiCA CASP and Google-certified. X requires country-specific authorization; its published Portugal rule calls for proof of Bank of Portugal registration. TikTok applies market-specific restrictions and approval processes, and in several markets expressly disallows non-custodial-wallet advertising. Therefore, do not treat ad-account approval as a launch assumption.

When approved, sequence paid tests:

1. branded search protection;
2. exact/high-intent search around supported jobs;
3. retargeting of consented/eligible site visitors where allowed;
4. whitelisted creator content;
5. broad social only after the first four show retained-customer economics.

## 8. Creative growth concepts

### 1. Aurel onchain operating review

A free, 20-minute founder-led review for the first 50 qualified applicants. Output: a plain-language map of wallets, liquidity, protocol/issuer concentration, signing/recovery practices, and operational friction. It is educational and descriptive, not individualized investment advice. It creates research, trust, conversion, and referral material at once.

### 2. Wallet/treasury checkup

A privacy-first public tool that reads a user-supplied address or locally connected wallet and produces:

- chain and asset fragmentation;
- stablecoin issuer concentration;
- protocol exposure;
- immediately liquid versus externally deployed assets;
- known unlimited approvals;
- recovery/security checklist; and
- an exportable “questions to review” report.

The report must explain data freshness and limitations, avoid scoring a person as financially safe, and avoid action recommendations until the relevant legal/product framework is approved. The CTA is to apply for guided access.

### 3. “What will I sign?” transaction receipt

Turn Aurel's simulation and policy engine into a clear shareable receipt: control, destination, amount, fees, approvals, dependencies, and what could fail. Public examples can be anonymized and used as short-form content. This makes the security model tangible.

### 4. The stablecoin paycheck operating guide

An interactive guide for people paid partly in USDC: reserve planning, wallet separation, records, tax questions to ask, safe movement, and jurisdiction/provider limits. Partner with a payroll company and a tax professional. Avoid tax advice; localize only with qualified reviewers.

### 5. “Where your digital dollars live” series

A visual series explaining issuer, wallet, protocol, bridge, chain, and card-program boundaries. Each episode answers one question in under 45 seconds and links to a deeper source page. This directly supports Aurel's differentiation and is harder to commoditize than market commentary.

### 6. Founding 100 ledger

With explicit consent, publish anonymized product improvements driven by the first 100 customers: problem, change, verification, and date. Do not publish balances or vanity volume. This turns careful beta operations into social proof.

### 7. Public trust changelog

Extend the existing status/trust center with an effective-dated security, provider, risk, and incident changelog. A calm monthly “what changed and why” memo is a distribution asset for customers, partners, and journalists.

### 8. Private onchain money clinics

Run small clinics with one founder, one qualified accountant/security professional, and 8–12 target users. Attendees bring workflows, not assets. The session yields research, partner trust, clips, FAQs, and qualified applications without a conference sponsorship budget.

## 9. Content and short-form video system

### Content pillars

1. **Operate:** receiving stablecoins, moving across chains, records, liquidity, and recurring workflows.
2. **Protect:** signing, approvals, recovery, wallet separation, counterparties, and incident response.
3. **Understand:** custody/provider boundaries, stablecoin issuers, protocols, bridges, fees, and liquidity.
4. **Build:** product decisions, transparent limitations, security work, and customer-driven improvements.
5. **Use:** short, real product demonstrations with exact availability and risk context.

Avoid daily price commentary, token predictions, generic AI news, motivational founder content, and sensational “banking is dead” claims.

### Sustainable cadence for a lean team

- 1 source memo or recorded founder conversation per week;
- 3 short videos per week across YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, X, and relevant Base social surfaces;
- 4–5 text/visual posts per week, derived from the same source;
- 1 product/security deep dive every two weeks;
- 1 live clinic or office hour per month;
- 1 trust/product changelog email per month.

Do not create unique material for every network. One researched source should generate the week's assets.

### Short-form format

```text
0–2 seconds: name the expensive or frightening problem
2–8 seconds: show the concrete artifact or result
8–30 seconds: explain one mechanism with a real screen or diagram
30–40 seconds: state the limitation/risk
40–45 seconds: invite the next useful action
```

Example hooks:

- “Your USDC balance is not the same thing as available cash.”
- “Before you sign a bridge transaction, check these three dependencies.”
- “A wallet can be self-custodial and your card can still be frozen. Here is why.”
- “If your income arrives in stablecoins, this is the operating system you probably built in spreadsheets.”
- “This approval can outlive the transaction you meant to make.”

Use real founders or subject-matter experts on camera. Product screens, whiteboard explanations, and annotated transactions will build more trust than synthetic presenters. YouTube's own guidance emphasizes delivering the title/thumbnail promise immediately and using retention data to improve openings. Its current policy requires disclosure for realistic, meaningfully altered or generated media; script assistance and minor production edits do not by themselves require that disclosure.

### AI-assisted content workflow

1. Record one founder/customer-research conversation or draft one evidence-backed memo.
2. Transcribe and extract questions, objections, hooks, and quotable insights.
3. Retrieve current facts only from the approved product docs, provider docs, chain data, and regulatory sources.
4. Generate channel-specific drafts and 3–5 hook variants.
5. Run an automated claim check against the approved claims register and banned-phrase list.
6. Human-review every claim, disclosure, visual, and call to action.
7. Produce captions, cuts, thumbnails, translations, and metadata.
8. Publish with a campaign/content ID.
9. Feed retention, saves, qualified visits, applications, and activation back to the topic database.

AI should reduce editing and repurposing cost, not manufacture expertise or testimonials. Never upload customer data, wallet-linked identity, support cases, unpublished incidents, or provider-confidential material to a general-purpose model.

### Content metrics

Rank content by:

1. qualified application rate;
2. partner or customer conversations created;
3. saves/shares and high-intent replies;
4. 7- and 30-day assisted activation;
5. audience retention and return viewers;
6. raw reach.

Views are diagnostic, not the goal.

## 10. Funnel, metrics, and unit economics

### Canonical funnel

```text
qualified visitor
  -> application started
  -> qualified application
  -> invitation issued
  -> invitation accepted
  -> identity + passkey + wallet ready
  -> first live balance or connected wallet
  -> first reviewed/signed action
  -> 30-day retained funded relationship
  -> paying or contribution-positive relationship
  -> qualified referral
```

### Definitions

- **Qualified application:** approved geography plus a supported, repeated job and willingness to test safely.
- **Activated customer:** completes identity/access setup, secures the account, sees a live balance/connected wallet, and completes the cohort's defined first-value action.
- **Retained relationship:** returns and repeats a meaningful workflow or maintains a genuinely used funded relationship in the relevant 30-day window.
- **CAC:** all campaign spend, creator/partner fees, incentives, attributable tools, agency cost, and attributable sales/onboarding labor divided by activated or retained customers. Always report both CAC-to-activation and CAC-to-30-day-retention.
- **Contribution margin:** subscription, disclosed service fees, interchange share, and other realized revenue minus provider, network, rewards, benefits, disputes/fraud, support, and variable infrastructure cost.

### North-star and guardrails

North-star: **30-day retained funded relationships that complete a meaningful workflow.**

Guardrails:

- serious security incidents and unexplained financial-state breaks;
- complaint and account-compromise rate;
- support minutes and cost per activated customer;
- failed/stale transaction rate;
- concentration by country, channel, provider, asset, and protocol;
- incentive/referral abuse;
- opt-out, spam complaint, and creator-claim violation rates.

### CAC discipline

Do not set a paid CAC target from competitor anecdotes. The repository's economic inputs are still blank. Use:

```text
maximum fully loaded CAC
  = expected monthly contribution margin
  x target payback months
  x retention confidence factor
```

During beta, use a six-month payback ceiling and a conservative retention factor of 50–70% until cohorts mature. Example only: if realized contribution margin is $15 per month, a six-month ceiling is $90 before applying the confidence factor, producing a test ceiling of $45–$63. If contribution margin is not observed, paid CAC capacity is unknown—not unlimited.

Each channel receives a small fixed test budget and a kill/iterate/scale decision. Scale only when 30-day retained CAC and quality guardrails are credible.

### Experiment card

Every experiment should record:

- target segment and problem;
- hypothesis;
- offer/message;
- channel and exact audience;
- cost and staff time cap;
- primary conversion and guardrails;
- minimum evidence and run length;
- result by retained cohort, not just leads;
- decision and next test.

## 11. Growth tooling to build

The current system is strong after authentication but incomplete before it. Build the minimum first-party growth layer instead of buying a complex marketing stack.

### P0 — before public waitlist

1. **Waitlist applications**
   - status, country, use case, fit band, consent version, reviewer, decision, and timestamps;
   - encrypted/minimized personal data and a deletion/retention rule;
   - no wallet address requirement.
2. **Attribution touches**
   - first/last UTM, referrer, landing page, partner code, content ID, and anonymous session ID;
   - join to a customer only after consent/authentication;
   - strip sensitive URL parameters and never put wallet addresses in analytics.
3. **Referral model**
   - referrer, named/expiring invite, issuance reason, acceptance, activation, retention, reward status, and fraud flags.
4. **Public funnel events**
   - landing viewed, product tour viewed, application started/submitted/qualified, invite issued/accepted, onboarding milestones, first value, and retention milestones.
5. **Claims and creative register**
   - approved phrase, evidence URL/version, geography, product state, required disclosure, approver, review date, and assets using it.

### P1 — during Cohort 1

1. Cohort dashboard showing conversion time, drop-off, activation, first action, retention, support cost, and incidents by source.
2. Lifecycle messaging with explicit consent, templates, frequency caps, transactional/marketing separation, and stop rules when access is restricted.
3. Partner links/codes and a partner portal limited to aggregate, privacy-safe cohort status.
4. Experiment registry and content IDs connected to applications and retained activation.
5. Feedback synthesis that clusters themes but always links summaries back to the original, access-controlled feedback.

### P2 — after repeatability

1. Referral unlocks and reward accounting.
2. Content copilot grounded only in approved Aurel/product/provider sources.
3. Automated pre-publication checks for banned claims, missing disclosures, stale screenshots, unsupported countries/features, and AI/sponsorship labels.
4. Treasury/wallet checkup and shareable risk receipts.
5. Incrementality and holdout support for paid campaigns.

Do not build a custom CRM, email-delivery service, social scheduler, or video generator. Integrate replaceable vendors behind Aurel-owned event and consent models.

## 12. First 90 days

### Days 1–14: foundations

- Name one founder as GTM owner and one legal/compliance claims approver.
- Choose the first proposed country and segment for legal/provider review.
- Conduct the first 10 customer interviews.
- Define activation and first value for that segment.
- Draft the claims register and prohibited-claims list.
- Design the waitlist/application, consent, attribution, and referral schema.
- Replace the primary landing CTA in the approved launch copy.
- Build a list of 50 high-fit prospects and 20 potential distribution partners.

### Days 15–30: proof assets

- Complete 10 more customer and 5 professional-referrer interviews.
- Run the five-user acceptance cohort.
- Produce the first source memo, six short videos, and one deep technical explainer.
- Create the onchain operating review template.
- Publish or update the fee/provider/security/status pages needed for every acquisition claim.
- Secure three partner pilot commitments.
- Instrument the complete application-to-activation funnel.

### Days 31–60: founding 25

- Invite five users per week.
- Hold weekly activation, reliability, feedback, support, and channel reviews.
- Run the first two private money clinics.
- Launch the fixed-cadence content engine.
- Test three positioning variants against qualified application rate, not click-through alone.
- Prepare the wallet/treasury checkup specification; build only after recurring demand is visible.
- Request integration listings or joint educational sessions from relevant ecosystems.

### Days 61–90: repeatability decision

- Complete the 25-user cohort and four-week retention read.
- Identify the single best segment/workflow and the top two acquisition channels.
- Stop channels that create unqualified applications or costly support.
- Launch named customer referrals only if activation and trust thresholds are met.
- Start one small creator pilot and one partner-specific cohort.
- Model actual contribution margin and a maximum CAC.
- Decide whether to proceed toward 100 users, remain at 25 while fixing the product, or pause the affected path.

## 13. Team and outside-firm plan

### Now

The minimum team is:

- founder/GTM owner: positioning, interviews, partnerships, sales, and public voice;
- product growth engineer: waitlist, attribution, referral, experiments, and dashboard;
- fractional compliance/fintech counsel: countries, claims, promotions, creators, referrals, and partner structure;
- part-time content producer/editor: recording, editing, design, and distribution;
- product/operations owner: activation, support, safety, and cohort review.

### Agency decision

Do not retain a broad crypto marketing agency before Cohort 1. An agency can manufacture reach before Aurel knows which segment retains, while adding claim and reputation risk.

Hire specialists by work package:

- positioning/customer research facilitator, only if founder interviews stall;
- video editor/producer for a defined weekly output;
- financial-services PR firm for a genuine milestone;
- performance agency only after ad eligibility and retained-CAC limits are established;
- lifecycle/CRM implementation specialist after the consent and event model exists.

### When a performance or growth firm becomes rational

All must be true:

- at least 100 activated customers or equivalent behavioral evidence;
- one segment has stable 60–90-day retention;
- acquisition claims and countries are approved;
- ad-platform eligibility is confirmed in writing;
- contribution margin and maximum CAC are known;
- first-party attribution is working;
- the founder can provide 10+ proven creative concepts and customer language.

### Procurement scorecard

Score candidates on regulated-finance experience, evidence of retained-customer outcomes, creator/affiliate compliance, measurement and incrementality, data ownership, senior-team involvement, security/privacy, contract flexibility, and references. Reject firms that lead with token promotion, paid shill networks, guaranteed placements, follower counts, undisclosed influencers, or asset/volume-based compensation.

Use a 6–8 week paid pilot with a fixed scope, Aurel-owned accounts/data, no long lock-in, and success defined as qualified and retained activation—not impressions or waitlist size.

## 14. Budget allocation

### Pre-Cohort 1

Keep cash spend low and spend founder time heavily:

- customer research and small clinics;
- legal/claims review;
- part-time editing/design;
- basic lifecycle/analytics vendors;
- no broad paid media and no expensive launch event.

### Cohort 1 to 2

Use a capped experimental pool rather than a standing media budget. A practical lean envelope is €5,000–€15,000 across 90 days, excluding core team and legal/provider work:

- 35% specialist content production;
- 25% small creator/newsletter/podcast pilots;
- 20% private clinics and partner assets;
- 10% tools and measurement;
- 10% reserve for the winning channel.

Release the reserve only after a test produces qualified, activated users. No channel receives more than 25% of the experimental pool before it has a retained-customer read.

### Incentives

Use at most a small, fixed portion of expected contribution margin and budget it at the moment of referral. Prefer access and service rewards. Never subsidize yield, promise uncapped cashback, or reward balances that produce no revenue.

## 15. Regulatory and platform constraints

This plan is not legal advice. Before soliciting customers in a country, obtain activity-by-activity advice covering Aurel, its regulated partners, its content, referrers, creators, waitlist, and exact calls to action.

Important current constraints:

- MiCA requires client/prospect information and marketing communications by crypto-asset service providers to be fair, clear, and not misleading. Article 59 also prevents an unauthorized person from using a name or marketing that suggests it is a crypto-asset service provider.
- The EU supervisory authorities advise customers to check whether a provider is authorized and warn that protection can be limited depending on the asset/service.
- Platform ad approval is not equivalent to legal permission, and legal permission is not equivalent to platform approval.
- Paid creators, affiliates, partner emails, founder posts, events, and referral pages can all become marketing communications. Archive the content, approval, audience/country, disclosure, and evidence.
- Disclose sponsorships and realistic synthetic media using each platform's tools. Do not use synthetic customer testimonials or celebrity likenesses.
- Separate product education from personalized advice. Do not present a protocol or allocation as suitable for a person without the appropriate framework and information.

## 16. Immediate decisions for the founders

The plan can begin before these answers, but they determine the first campaign:

1. Which single country will counsel/provider evaluate first?
2. Is the first commercial wedge an individual stablecoin operator or a small-company treasury principal?
3. Which workflow counts as first value: connected portfolio, received USDC, safe send, cross-chain move, or Earn action?
4. Who owns the public founder voice and can sustain one weekly source recording?
5. What support hours and response commitment can the first 25 customers actually receive?
6. Which revenue line is expected to support CAC first: membership, disclosed service fee, interchange, or another approved line?

Until those are decided, the correct action is customer research, partner development, trust content, funnel instrumentation, and readiness work—not traffic buying.
