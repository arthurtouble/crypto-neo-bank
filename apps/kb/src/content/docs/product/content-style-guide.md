title: Aurel content standard
description: Internal writing and claims standard for product, support, documentation, and marketing.
---

This guide keeps Aurel clear, calm, and recognizably human. It applies to the product, documentation, support messages, and legal summaries.

## Rules

1. Start with the thing the reader needs to know or do.
2. Use familiar verbs: send, receive, review, sign, save, pause.
3. Keep one idea per sentence. Most interface sentences should stay below 18 words.
4. Name the real system when it matters: Privy, Base, Aave, or the customer’s wallet.
5. Do not turn implementation language into customer language. Avoid “control plane,” “intent,” “orchestration,” “provider-gated,” “posture,” and “rail.”
6. Do not narrate the interface. Labels should say what an action does.
7. State limits beside the decision they affect. Do not bury them in a generic disclaimer.
8. Use sentence case. Avoid slogans, stacked adjectives, and claims we cannot prove.
9. Say “you” for the customer and “we” for Aurel. Use “customer” only in operational or legal text.
10. Keep precise terms when precision protects the reader. Explain them once in plain language.

## Rewrite prompt

> Rewrite this for Aurel, a calm financial product. Preserve every factual limit and warning. Lead with what the reader needs. Use plain English, active voice, sentence case, and short sentences. Remove filler, hype, repeated ideas, implementation jargon, and instructions that the interface should make obvious. Prefer concrete nouns and familiar verbs. Do not add claims, reassurance, urgency, or friendliness that the source does not support. Keep legal meaning intact. Return only the revised copy.

## Review checklist

- Can someone understand the action without helper text?
- Is the first sentence the most useful one?
- Is every claim supported by the current product?
- Does the risk appear before confirmation, not after it?
- Could any label be two words shorter?

The standard follows GOV.UK’s interface-writing guidance: start with less, put important words first, use one idea per sentence, and fix the interface before explaining it with copy.
