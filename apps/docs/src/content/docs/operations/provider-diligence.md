title: Provider diligence
description: The evidence package for Bridge, Rain, card, benefit, and other financial-service partners.

A sandbox account proves technical access, not production approval. Aurel expects a provider to diligence the platform, founders, product flows, customers, countries, controls, volumes, and operating capacity.

## Package contents

Aurel maintains:

- architecture and source-of-truth rules;
- fund and data-flow diagrams;
- threat model and internal security review;
- external security-review scope;
- compliance and operating responsibility matrix;
- provider requirements scorecard;
- incident, webhook, queue, recovery, and release runbooks;
- data-retention schedule;
- private-beta cohorts, limits, and stop conditions;
- mainnet acceptance protocol;
- volume and unit-economics input model;
- legal and jurisdiction decision register.

## Questions for every provider

The provider must confirm customer contracting party, approved countries, identity and enhanced-review ownership, supported assets and networks, safeguarding, settlement and reversals, account and card behavior, signed events, idempotency, reconciliation exports, disputes, incident escalation, data roles, security evidence, full pricing, reserves, minimums, and termination.

## Technical demonstration

The partner demo shows customer authentication, wallet authority, policy controls, source-of-truth labels, transaction states, provider-event verification, retries, dead-letter handling, reconciliation, feature shutdown, status communication, and recovery from an empty projection database.

Simulated fiat and card adapters are identified as simulations. They are not presented as active provider services.

## Decision rule

A provider is not selected on API breadth alone. Its contract and production program must support Aurel's exact use case, countries, customer relationship, failure model, evidence needs, and economics. Written answers override sales assumptions.
